Home Buyers

The households who want to buy a CLT home: who they are, whether they are ready to buy, their applications, the waiting list, and how the trust chooses one household for each home.

Projects: src/CommonGround.HomeBuyers, src/CommonGround.HomeBuyers.Contracts. Last checked against the code: 2026-11-13. Read the Architecture Overview and the Shared Kernel page first.

1. What it is

The CLT idea behind it. A CLT sells its homes only to households below an income limit, set as a percent of area median income (AMI). HUD or a state agency publishes the dollar limits each year, per household size. Before buying, a household must be ready: it has done housing counseling and the trust's orientation, and a lender has prequalified it for a mortgage. An approved household joins the waiting list. When a home comes up, staff choose one household from the list and must be able to show a funder why they chose it.

What it owns:

  • Households: the members and their contact details, annual income, whether they need an accessible home, and the three readiness steps.
  • Homeowner applications: a household's request to buy, from submission to approval (the waiting list) to selection for a home and the closed purchase.
  • Home selections: staff choosing one household on the waiting list for one home, by stated priorities or by lottery.
  • Income limit schedules: the published dollar limits, per percent of AMI and household size, from an effective date.

2. The model

Four aggregates, all deciders. Homes belong to Property Registry, so a home is a string ID here, and its facts are a local copy (HomeFactsView).

ID prefixes: household-, household-member-, application-, selection-, incomelimitschedule-.

Household

  • Household size is the number of members. A household has exactly one primary member, who can't be removed. The primary member is the buyer named on the purchase.
  • Readiness is complete when counseling, orientation and a prequalification are all on record. An expired prequalification still counts for readiness. Selection separately checks that it is valid on the selection date.
  • Recording a prequalification schedules a message that marks it expired at 00:00 UTC on its expiry date. No decision waits for that message: validity is always worked out from ExpiryDate.

HomeownerApplication

  • On submission the application copies the household's size, income and accessibility need (Declaration). While it is Submitted or Approved it follows the household: HouseholdChangedHandler handles HouseholdCircumstancesUpdated, HouseholdMemberAdded and HouseholdMemberRemoved, loads the household as it is now, and calls FollowHousehold with its current Declare(). It reads current state rather than the event, so a late or repeated message still leaves the application matching the household, and an unchanged declaration decides nothing. A Selected application keeps the figures it was chosen on; Homeownership checks eligibility before closing. If the purchase falls through, PurchaseFellThroughHandler returns the application and then has it follow the household, so a change made during the purchase marks it. Readiness is read from the household when it is compared and when it is chosen.
  • When an Approved application's income or size changes, ApplicationDeclarationUpdated carries a QualificationToConfirmMark: the date of the first change and the income and size staff last checked (at approval or the last confirmation). Later changes keep that date and those figures, so staff confirm once against the latest. If the figures go back to exactly the checked ones the mark clears; any other change, a drop in income too, keeps it, because qualifying also checks the income is enough for a mortgage. A change to the accessibility need alone marks nothing. ConfirmQualification clears the mark (ApplicationQualificationConfirmed), dated no later than today and no earlier than the change. The application stays Approved throughout, so its approval date and place on the waiting list don't change.
  • A household can have one open application. Selected counts as open (ApplicationOpen).
  • The waiting list is every Approved application, oldest approval first (OnWaitingList). There is no separate list to maintain.

  • Staff can't deny or withdraw a Selected application. It leaves Selected only through the purchase in Homeownership. When a purchase falls through, the application goes back with its original approval date, so the household keeps its place.

IncomeLimitSchedule

  • The schedule in effect on a day is the one with the latest EffectiveFrom on or before it. Two schedules can't share a date.
  • Each level holds the printed limits for 1 to 8 people. IncomeLimitLevel.LimitFor works out larger households the way HUD does. Both types are in the Shared Kernel because Homeownership stores the same table on a purchase.

  • HomeRegistered creates the HomeFactsView copy: label, parcel, bedrooms, accessibility and income limit. HomeIncomeLimitSet updates it, and fails to the dead letter queue if the copy is missing, rather than leave selection on the old limit. HomeSaleStageChanged keeps the home's sale stage on the copy, so a household is chosen only for a home that is offered.
  • ApplicationSelected starts the purchase in Homeownership. It carries the buyer's contact details, the price, the ground lease template, and the income, household size and limits the household was judged by, so the purchase records why the buyer qualified.
  • The purchase ApplicationSelected starts is what stops the home being leased by hand. Property Registry uses it for home availability, and publishes the home's new stage back.
  • PurchaseClosed and PurchaseFellThrough do nothing when the purchase has no ApplicationId: staff started it directly, without a selection.

4. Choosing a household

Funders audit this part, and it changes three aggregates without one transaction.

  • One step, from the waiting list as it is. There is no start and nothing is snapshotted ahead of time. WaitingListComparisonLoader compares every application on the waiting list on the date of the choice against the home, from the inline read models, for both the selection page and the two commands. WaitingListComparison gives each the home's dollar limit for its household size (from the home's percent of AMI and the schedule in effect on the date) and every CannotBeChosenReason, including QualificationToConfirm while the application is marked. Incomes and sizes are the application's, which follow the household. An application approved after the date wasn't waiting yet and is left out.
  • A selection is written once. HouseholdSelectedByPriority or HouseholdSelectedByLottery starts the stream and is its only event. It records the whole waiting list (RecordedApplication), each marked Chosen, PassedOver or CouldNotBeChosen with its reasons, and the schedule as evidence. Nothing changes it afterwards: whether the purchase closed or fell through is the purchase's.
  • The home's sale stage says whether a household can be chosen, not an old selection. Property Registry publishes HomeSaleStageChanged and HomeSaleStageChangedHandler keeps the newest on HomeFactsView. A choice is refused unless the home is Offered. Recording a choice also sets HomeFactsView.LatestSelectionId, and the home counts as chosen until a stage arrives that accounts for that selection, so a buyback or a purchase falling through offers it again and a second choice before then is refused.
  • Two choices at the same moment. HomeFactsView is stored with optimistic concurrency, so the second of two choices for the same home fails, retries, and finds the home chosen. One household chosen for two homes at once is caught by the application stream's version.
  • Choosing is refused when the date is after today or before the home became available, when nobody is waiting, when the home has a limit the schedule in effect has no level for (Today shows that as income-limits-missing), or when the chosen household can't be chosen on the date. Priorities (SelectionPriority) rank households that qualify. They never decide who qualifies. Choosing by priority needs at least one priority and a written reason. HomeownerApplication.SelectForHome checks the chosen household's own aggregates too, and refuses an application with a qualification to confirm.
  • The lottery can be re-run. It records its seed, the households found tied (if any) and the pool, and draws with SplitMix64, not System.Random, whose seeded sequence can change between .NET versions. The pool leaves out anyone who can't be chosen on the date, so the winner always can be. A tie needs at least two households.
  • Choosing writes three documents. The selection, the home's facts and the chosen application, through HomeSelectionChoice. PassOverCandidatesHandler then sends one PassOverApplication per application the choice marked passed over, so choices for different homes can be recorded at the same time. A household that couldn't be chosen isn't passed over: it never lost to anyone. HouseholdPassOversView keeps the history per household, across applications.
  • A purchase that falls through starts nothing. PurchaseFellThroughHandler returns the application to the waiting list with its approval date, and it takes the household's current figures, marked for staff to confirm if its income or size changed during the purchase. The home is offered again through Property Registry, and staff choose the next household.

Unconfirmed with a practitioner: whether a trust holds a home for one household before choosing, or fixes the list a committee or board decides from. Neither is modelled. Also unconfirmed: whether a trust re-checks a waiting household that reports a change, whether a marked household should be blocked from being chosen or only flagged, and whether the mark should clear on its own when the figures go back to the ones checked (issue #23).

6. Gaps

  • A purchase that staff start directly, without a selection, isn't checked against any income limit.
  • The application follows the household through a message, so it can lag the household by a moment after staff save a change.
  • The prequalification expiry message runs at 00:00 UTC on the expiry date, not in the trust's time zone, and on the last day the prequalification is still valid.

7. Changing this context

  • A new reason a household can't be chosen: add a CannotBeChosenReason, check it in WaitingListComparison (loading what it needs in WaitingListComparisonLoader), refuse it in HomeownerApplication.SelectForHome, and give it words in the web app's cannotBeChosenReasonLabel. The lottery pool and pass-overs follow the comparison.
  • A new fact the selection judges by: put it on WaitingApplication and RecordedApplication, so the record shows what the choice was made from, and carry it on ApplicationSelected so the purchase keeps it.
  • A new question about the waiting list: query with OnWaitingList. Don't compare Phase strings in a new place.
