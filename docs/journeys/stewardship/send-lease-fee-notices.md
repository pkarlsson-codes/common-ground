# Send Lease Fee Notices

For the trust's regular round of late notices: print every letter that is due, post them, and record them as sent.

1. User navigates to Stewardship, clicks the Lease Fees tab, and switches the filter to "Notice due" (or follows a Lease fee notice item on Today to its lease, see [Record Lease Fee Notice](../ground-leases/record-lease-fee-notice.md))
2. The table lists each active lease whose oldest unpaid charge has passed the trust's notice days (see [Set Lease Fee Notice Policy](../trust-policy/set-lease-fee-notice-policy.md)) and that has not had that notice since its arrears began. The Notice column shows "First due", "Second due" or "Notice of default due"
3. User checks the list: a household that has paid but whose payment isn't recorded yet is fixed by recording the payment first (see [Record Lease Fee Payment](../ground-leases/record-lease-fee-payment.md)), which takes it off the list
4. User clicks "Print Notices". A print page opens in a new tab with one letter per lease, a page each, and the browser's print dialog
5. Each letter has today's date, the homeowner's name, the home, the heading "Lease fee past due" (first), "Second notice: lease fee past due" (second) or "Notice of default: lease fee unpaid" (notice of default), the amount due, the months unpaid and the monthly fee, a request to pay or get in touch, and the trust's sign-off. On a lease that charges interest: when interest is owed, the amount due includes it and the letter says how much ("This includes $X of interest at 10% a year on fees not paid within 30 days of their due date."); while the oldest unpaid fee is still within the waiver days, the letter says interest is counted from the due date and won't be charged if that month's fee is paid by the waiver date. The second notice also says that continued non-payment can lead to a formal notice of default under the ground lease. A notice of default replaces the request to pay with the cure terms: "This is a formal notice of default under your ground lease. To cure the default, you must pay the $X owed within 30 days of the date of this notice, by {date}."; when the trust gives an extension, "If you pay at least two-thirds of this amount ($Y) by {date}, you will have 30 more days, until {date}, to pay the rest."; and that the trust may end the lease or buy the home if it isn't cured in time. Its days are the trust's cure period, counted from the letter's date, so it should be sent and recorded that day. The letter names no lease section, since trusts number their leases differently. A notice of default is followed by one copy per current permitted mortgage on the lease (Exhibit: Permitted Mortgages, B.6): the same letter headed "Copy to {lender}" with the lender's notice address, or "Notice address not on file" in red so staff find it before posting
6. User posts or hands over the letters, then clicks "Record Notices Sent"
7. "Record Notices Sent" panel opens with every lease with a notice due, each checked, showing homeowner, notice level and amount. User unticks any not sent, and sets Date Sent (default today) and Delivery (First-Class Mail, Certified Mail, Registered Mail, Hand Delivered or Email; default First-Class Mail). While a notice of default is ticked, the delivery must be Certified Mail, Registered Mail or Hand Delivered; otherwise the record button is disabled and the panel says "Notices of default must go by certified or registered mail, or by hand. Choose one of those, or untick them and record them separately."
8. User clicks "Record N Notices", or "Cancel"
9. Toast: "N notices recorded". A notice of default keeps the lenders it was copied to (the lease's current permitted mortgages on the date sent). The leases leave the "Notice due" list, and their Notice column shows the notice sent and its date
10. If a notice can't be recorded (for example the lease was paid up in the meantime), the panel stays open and lists which leases failed and why; the rest are recorded
